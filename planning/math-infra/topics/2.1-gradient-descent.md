**Prerequisites:** [] · **relatedTo:** `math-infra/gradients-and-bandwidth`, `math-infra/precision-and-memory`

---

## TLDR — why / what / how

**Why.** Every operational fact about training — why jobs run for *weeks*, why they checkpoint, why
there's "optimizer state" eating HBM, why a run sometimes diverges into `NaN`s — falls out of one idea:
**training is gradient descent.** An infra engineer who gets the *shape* of that loop (without any
calculus) can reason about run length, stability failures, and resource footprint instead of treating
"the model trains" as a black box. This is the general ML-math foundation the whole track was missing.

**What.** What "learning" is, in systems terms: a **loss** (one number: how wrong the model is), a
**gradient** (which way is downhill — *no derivative required to use it*), a **step** in that direction,
repeated **thousands of times**. The **learning rate** sets step size: too big → **overshoot / diverge /
oscillate**; too small → **crawls** (wasted compute). **Momentum** smooths the path. Why it matters to
infra: it's an **iterative loop**, not a one-shot compute — hence long-lived jobs, checkpoints, and
per-parameter bookkeeping (the optimizer state that M2.2 budgets).

**How.** NEW sim **`gradient-descent-explorer`**: a 2-D **loss landscape** (contours) with a marker that
**steps downhill**; sliders for **learning rate** and **momentum**; a side **loss-vs-step curve** that
shows the three regimes — converge, oscillate, diverge. Hard-Knocks: a run's loss is exploding to `NaN`
— decide whether the **learning rate is too high** (tune it down to converge) or the data's bad.

## Accuracy gates (intuition-level; no derivations; confirm framing at draft)

- **Gradient** = the direction of steepest *increase* of the loss; descent steps the **opposite** way.
  We *use* it as "downhill," never compute it. (The how-it's-computed is L300+ / out of scope.)
- **Learning rate (η)** = step-size multiplier. There's a **stability band**: above it the loss
  diverges/oscillates; far below it converges but slowly. (Frame as a tuning knob, like a control gain.)
- **Many small steps:** training = thousands-to-millions of iterations over mini-batches — *this* is why
  training is long-running and checkpointed, not a single matmul.
- **Momentum / Adam** carry running averages of past gradients to smooth and speed convergence — which
  is *why* an optimizer keeps extra per-parameter state (the M2.2 tee-up; don't derive Adam).
- **Local minima / non-convexity:** real loss surfaces are bumpy; mention at altitude (the landscape has
  valleys), don't go deep.
- Keep it framework-agnostic: this is general ML/AI math (applies to GPUs too), not TPU-specific.

## Learning objectives

1. Describe training as **loss → gradient (downhill) → step → repeat**, with zero calculus.
2. Explain the **learning rate** trade: too high diverges/oscillates, too low crawls; there's a band.
3. Connect the loop to operations: training is **iterative and long** → checkpoints, restarts, weeks of
   runtime are *inherent*, not incidental.
4. Explain why an **optimizer carries extra per-parameter state** (momentum/variance) — setting up the
   memory budget in M2.2.

---

## Must-learn spine (SRE bridge baked into each beat)

1. **Loss is a single scalar you're minimizing.** *SRE bridge:* an objective/error metric you're driving
   toward zero — like tuning a system to minimize p99 latency.
2. **Gradient descent is an iterative control loop.** Measure → step → re-measure. *SRE bridge:* a
   feedback controller / hill-climbing search; you don't jump to the answer, you converge to it.
3. **Learning rate is the gain — and it has a stability region.** *SRE bridge:* crank a control loop's
   gain too high and it **oscillates/blows up**; too low and it's sluggish. Same knob, same failure modes
   (the `NaN` blow-up = an unstable loop).
4. **It runs for a very long time, so state must survive.** *SRE bridge:* a long-running stateful job →
   you checkpoint progress so a crash doesn't restart from zero (ties straight to 2.3 checkpointing).

## Sim spec

### NEW — `gradient-descent-explorer` (two views in one)
**Concept (one):** the learning rate decides whether the loop converges, crawls, or explodes.
**Layout:** **(view A)** a 2-D loss surface drawn as contours with a marker that takes steps downhill
from a start point; **(view B)** a synced **loss-vs-step line chart**. Sliders: **learning rate**
(small → tiny steps, slow descent; medium → smooth convergence; large → overshoot/oscillate; very large
→ **diverge off the surface**) and **momentum** (smooths zig-zag). A "Step" / "Run" control animates the
descent; the curve shows the regime. **The aha:** push the LR up and watch the same loop flip from a
clean glide into oscillation, then divergence — and the loss curve mirror it. **Standard:** real
`createSim`, tokens + light/dark, `prefers-reduced-motion` (skip-to-end-state), **deterministic** (fixed
surface + fixed seed; no `Math.random` in visuals), one `sim.checkpoint("observe-divergence")` the first
time the learner drives the loss to diverge (or to converge — pick the clearest "observed it" moment).

*(No 2nd reused sim — this concept is general ML math with no existing analog; the two synced views ARE
the multiple-angle depth.)*
