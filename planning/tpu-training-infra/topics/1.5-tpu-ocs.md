## TLDR — why / what / how

**Why.** OCS is the path's most magical idea — a pod that rewires itself by tilting
microscopic mirrors — and so far it's only a noun in 1.2/1.4. This topic gives it a
home: *how* steering light = changing topology, and what that buys (slicing,
healing, shapes).

**What.** The dual view: the logical topology software sees vs the Palomar MEMS
mirror matrix photons see. Five operations: global ring · pod slicing (isolation by
physics) · cross-rack adjacency (topology ≠ geography) · spare-cube repair (heal at
cube granularity, 4 mirror moves) · topology shuffle (circuits per-job, not
per-packet). The cube — not the chip — is the unit of reconfiguration.

**How.** The `tpu-ocs-explorer` sim (adapted from the user's "OCS Academy" artifact):
dual-pane canvas + controller telemetry + 5 modes + scripted failure/repair.
**De-gamified per the sim standard** — XP/missions/badge stripped; their questions
move into this topic's quiz; predict-then-observe becomes Steps. Deterministic
shuffle (fixed derangement cycle), no CDN fonts, listeners, `observe-ocs` checkpoint
when the spare-cube repair lands.

## Facts (verified vs the ISCA TPU v4 paper, arXiv:2304.01433)

- 4,096 chips/pod = 64 cubes of 64 (4×4×4); electrical inside a cube, optical between. ✓
- **48 optical in/out pairs per cube** to the OCS layer. ✓
- OCS layer ≈ **<5% of system cost and ~3% of power**. ✓ (original said "<5% power" — fixed)
- Mirror settle ≈ milliseconds → circuits set **per job**, never per packet. ✓
- No O-E-O conversion in the switch — rate-agnostic glass. ✓
