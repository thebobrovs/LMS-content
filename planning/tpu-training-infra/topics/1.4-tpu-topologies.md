## The "why v5p?" question → generalize the topic
`v5p` is a *chip name*, not a topology: TPU naming is `v{gen}{variant}` —
**`e` = efficiency** (inference-leaning), **`p` = performance** (training/pod-scaled);
the 8th gen renamed those variants to **`t`/`i`**. Topology spans generations
(2D torus, 3D torus, twisted torus, OCS-reconfigurable, Boardfly), so the topic
should teach **the networks and the shapes** with v5p as one worked example — not
be locked to v5p.

## Scope boundary
1.4 owns **the network**: how chips connect (ICI vs DCN), the **shapes** chips form,
and how shape drives **network diameter** and therefore **collective cost**. It does
*not* teach the collective algorithms themselves (that's 3.1) or model sharding
(3.2) — it gives the physical substrate they ride on.

## Why / What / How
- **Why:** Two slices with the *same chip count* train at very different speeds
  because of shape. An engineer who can't tell when a collective rides **ICI** vs the
  far-slower **DCN**, or why a long axis suits a ring all-reduce, will misplace
  parallelism and starve the chips on the network.
- **What:** The two networks (ICI vs DCN) and the topologies built on them — 2D/3D
  torus, twisted torus, OCS-reconfigurable slices, and 8i's **Boardfly**.
- **How:** Links wrap edges into a torus (low diameter); OCS reconfigures slices and
  routes around dead chips; Boardfly uses optical hierarchy to cut diameter further
  (~7 hops/1,024 chips vs 16 on a torus).

## Learning objectives
After this topic the learner can:
- Distinguish **ICI vs DCN** and identify which one a given collective runs over.
- Explain how **wrapping a grid into a torus** keeps network **diameter** low, and how diameter scales (≈ cube-root of chip count in 3D).
- Compare topologies across generations — **2D torus** (v5e/v6e), **3D torus** (v4/v5p/8t), **OCS-reconfigurable** (v4+), **Boardfly** (8i) — and say which bottleneck each addresses.
- Explain how a **twisted torus** makes an asymmetric slice (e.g. 4×4×8) **symmetric**, raising **bisection bandwidth (~70%)** — a real lever for global communication patterns.
- Distinguish **single-host slice** (one TPU VM) vs **multi-host slice** (many VMs over **ICI**) vs **Multislice** (many slices over **DCN**).
- **Pick a slice shape** for a workload (long axis → ring all-reduce / DP; balanced → tensor parallelism).

## Must-learn
1. **Two networks, three scopes:** **single-host slice** = one TPU VM; **multi-host slice** = many VMs over **ICI** (fast, intra-Pod, wraps into a torus); **Multislice** = many slices over **DCN** (slow). Keep tight, frequent communication on ICI. *(this is the distinction infra engineers conflate)*
2. **Shape dictates diameter dictates cost:** a torus keeps worst-case hops low (3D ≈ cube-root of N); the shape you rent is a real performance lever, not a detail.
3. **The twisted torus is a free win:** twisting an asymmetric slice (e.g. 4×4×8) makes it **symmetric**, raising **bisection bandwidth by up to ~70%** over a plain torus — big for global all-to-all / all-reduce.
4. **Topology evolved to break bottlenecks:** torus → OCS (reconfigure + route around failures, v4) → **Boardfly** (optical hierarchy, ~7 vs 16 hops, for inference collectives on 8i). *(ties back to 1.2 "chasing the bottleneck")*
