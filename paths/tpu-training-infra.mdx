For cloud-infrastructure engineers, SREs, and platform admins who know
distributed systems, Linux, and GKE, but are new to AI accelerators. By the end
you can design, orchestrate, scale, optimize, and operate Cloud TPU training
clusters — and predict the effect of a slice shape, a sharding plan, or a failed
chip on a real training run.

The path climbs four levels:

- **L100 — Foundations.** Build the mental model: how a TPU *computes* (the
  systolic array), how chips are packaged into slices and pods, how they wire
  together (ICI vs DCN, the 3D torus), and the JAX/XLA stack that drives them.
- **L200 — Single slice.** Run real work on one slice: orchestrate with XPK/GKE,
  understand SPMD multi-host execution, feed the chips (input pipeline), train
  with reference frameworks (MaxText / PyTorch-XLA), checkpoint with Orbax, and
  serve inference with vLLM.
- **L300 — Megascale.** Cross the single-slice boundary: multi-slice over DCN,
  3D parallelism and device-mesh sharding, the collectives underneath, and the
  performance engineering (MFU/HFU, profiling) that keeps utilization high.
- **L400 — Operate at hero scale.** Fault-tolerant, elastic training across tens
  of thousands of chips, custom Pallas kernels, in-memory recovery, inference
  quantization, and hardware-software co-design.

Each lesson pairs a browser simulation with a link to a hands-on Cloud TPU
codelab — learn the *why* here, then run it on real hardware.
